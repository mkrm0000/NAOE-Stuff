🔥 1. Thermal Efficiency General

Theory: The ratio of work done to heat supplied. It tells you how well an engine converts heat into useful work.

ηth = Work done Heat supplied = W Qs
Trick: "Work over heat" — what you get divided by what you paid for.
🌀 2. Otto Cycle Efficiency SI Engine

Theory: For a constant‑volume heat addition cycle (petrol engine), efficiency depends only on compression ratio r and specific heat ratio γ.

ηOtto = 1 − 1 rγ−1
Trick: "One minus one over r‑to‑the‑gamma‑minus‑one." Higher compression → higher efficiency.
⛽ 3. Diesel Cycle Efficiency CI Engine

Theory: For constant‑pressure heat addition (diesel cycle), efficiency depends on compression ratio r, cutoff ratio rc, and γ.

ηDiesel = 1 − 1 rγ−1 ⋅ rc−1 γ ( rc − 1 )
Trick: "Otto with a cutoff correction." Diesel is less efficient than Otto for same r.
❄️ 4. Carnot Efficiency Ideal Limit

Theory: Maximum possible efficiency between two temperature reservoirs. No real engine can beat it.

ηCarnot = 1 − TL TH
Trick: "One minus cold over hot." Temperatures in Kelvin!
⚙️ 5. Indicated Power (ip) Performance

Theory: Power developed inside the cylinder, based on mean effective pressure, swept volume, and cycles per second.

ip = pim L A n K 60000 (kW)
Trick: "P‑L‑A‑N‑K over 60k" — pressure × length × area × speed × cylinders ÷ 60,000.
🛞 6. Brake Power (bp) Performance

Theory: Useful power at the crankshaft. Measured with a dynamometer.

bp = 2 π N T 60000 (kW)
Trick: "Two pi N T over sixty thousand." Torque in N·m, speed in rpm.
🔗 7. Mechanical Efficiency Performance

Theory: Ratio of brake power to indicated power. Accounts for friction losses.

ηm = bp ip = bmep imep
Trick: "Brake over indicated." If no friction, ηm = 1.
🌬️ 8. Volumetric Efficiency Breathing

Theory: How well the engine fills the cylinder with fresh air compared to ideal.

ηv = m˙a / ρa Vdisp N / 2
Trick: "Actual air / ideal air." For 4‑stroke, N/2 because one intake per two revs.
🌡️ 9. Brake Thermal Efficiency Overall

Theory: How efficiently fuel energy is converted into brake power.

ηbth = bp E = bp m˙f × CV
Trick: "Brake power over fuel energy rate." CV = calorific value.
⛽ 10. Specific Fuel Consumption (sfc) Economy

Theory: Fuel consumed per unit power per unit time. Lower is better.

sfc = m˙f bp (kg/kWh)
Trick: "Fuel flow per brake power." Think of it as the engine's appetite.
📊 11. Mean Effective Pressure (mep) Pressure

Theory: Average pressure that would produce the same work if acting on the piston throughout the stroke.

pm = Work per cycle Vs = W Vs
Trick: "Work divided by swept volume." It's like average pressure pushing the piston.
🏃 12. Mean Piston Speed Kinematics

Theory: Average speed of the piston over one revolution. Important for wear and limiting engine speed.

sp = 2 L N
Trick: "Two times stroke times rpm." Piston goes up and down once per rev.
⚖️ 13. Equivalence Ratio (φ) Combustion

Theory: Ratio of actual fuel‑air ratio to stoichiometric fuel‑air ratio. φ = 1 is ideal.

φ = (F/A)actual (F/A)stoich
Trick: "Actual over stoichiometric." φ < 1 lean, φ > 1 rich.
💨 14. Inlet Valve Mach Index (Z) Gas Flow

Theory: Ratio of gas velocity to sonic velocity at the inlet valve. Limits volumetric efficiency when Z > 0.6.

Z = Ap Ai ⋅ Vp Ci α
Trick: "Piston area over inlet area × piston speed over sonic speed." Keep Z below 0.6 for good breathing.
🌊 15. Wave Power Renewable

Theory: Power per metre of wave crest. Proportional to wave height squared and period.

P = ρ g2 64 π H2 T ≈ 0.5 H2 T (kW/m)
Trick: "Half H‑squared T." The constant 0.5 kW/(m³·s) is magic for water waves.
📐 16. Air‑Standard Efficiency Ideal Cycle

Theory: Efficiency of an ideal cycle using air as the working fluid. Used as a benchmark.

ηair‑std = 1 − 1 rγ−1 (Otto)
Trick: Same as Otto formula. Only depends on compression ratio and γ.
